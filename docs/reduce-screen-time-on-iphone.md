## 1. Start with the built-in tools (free, but leaky)

Apple's **Screen Time** (Settings → Screen Time) lets you set **App Limits** and
**Downtime**. It's a fine first step, but on your own phone it's easy to tap past
— one "Ignore Limit" and you're back in. Turn it on anyway; it raises a small
speed bump.

**Focus modes** (Settings → Focus) can silence notifications and hide
distracting Home Screen pages. Pair a "Focus" mode with a schedule so your phone
gets quieter automatically in the evening.

## 2. Add a real app blocker with no easy bypass

Native limits fail because *you* control the off switch. A dedicated blocker
like **Detach** enforces blocks through the same **Family Controls / Screen Time
layer** parental controls use — but with **no in-app "5 more minutes" button**.
It's free, iOS 17+, and needs no account.

> yourself. No app can make that literally impossible. What Detach does is remove
> the *convenient* escape hatches — which is what actually breaks the habit for
> most people.

## 4. Automate it so you don't have to decide daily

Decision fatigue kills good intentions. Automate the block:

- **Scheduled auto-block** — lock social apps every weekday 9–5. See
  [`../shortcuts/scheduled-auto-block.md`](../shortcuts/scheduled-auto-block.md).
- **Wind-down routine** — block feeds and dim the phone every night. See
  [`../shortcuts/wind-down-routine.md`](../shortcuts/wind-down-routine.md).

## 5. Remove the triggers

- Turn off badge/notification dots for social apps (Settings → Notifications).
- Move distracting apps off your first Home Screen (or into the App Library).
- Log out of the worst offenders so re-entry takes effort.
